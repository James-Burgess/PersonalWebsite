## Dev discussion tonight

Goal: they walk away thinking "this person understands our problems better than
we do" — without feeling judged for the state of the codebase.

### Questions to ask

Pick 3 or 4. Let the conversation dictate which ones. Don't rattle off a list.

How are you deploying models to the Coral Edge TPU in production? I've been
experimenting with ONNX exports for MiewID and the latency profile looks good,
but I haven't tested on the actual hardware yet.

What's the biggest friction point for new contributors? The Java toolchain seems
like the obvious one but I'm curious if there's something deeper.

I noticed WBIA has a lot of inherited dependencies. Have you thought about a
clean rebuild, or is backwards compatibility with existing deployments the
priority?

The documentation gaps are pretty clear to me since I just onboarded myself.
What's the plan there? I have an API spec mostly written and a modernization
roadmap drafted.

How does the team split time between Sentinel hardware, NABIT firmware, and
Wildbook? Or are those separate teams entirely?

What's the relationship between the open-source Wildbook and the commercial
products? Is there a path from community contribution to production deployment?

### If they ask what you've been doing with Wildbook

Keep it casual. Don't list everything.

> I got curious about the re-identification pipeline and it kind of spiraled.
> I've touched the ML model, the Docker setup, the docs. Mostly things that made
> my own development experience less painful, which I figure helps everyone.

### If they mention the codebase quality

They know. Don't make them say it.

> There's a lot of history in there. Every codebase that survives a decade looks
> like that. The important thing is it works in production for real researchers.

### If your experience level comes up

> I'm not here to run anything. I spent years doing that and I'm happy to just
> build. The team has domain expertise I'll never have. I can fill the gaps they
> don't have time to fill.

### What not to say

Never: "Your system is a mess." "I rewrote your platform in a week." "I fixed
your model." "You clearly need me." "This should be easy to fix."

Never: compare yourself to anyone on their team.

Never: talk about salary or title.
